RIS-assisted Wi-Fi Sensing

Jun 5, 2024 RIS, Wi-Fi Sensing

As we all know, current Wi-Fi sesning systems exist many open issues, such as bad performance in NLoS and multi-user scenarios. Recently, Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS), as known as a promising technique in 6G communication, brings us new capabilities and is promising to make RF sensing systems more practical and robust.

RF sensing, especially Wi-Fi sensing, is very promising due to the privacy-persevering nature and the extensive adoption of Wi-Fi chips in IoT devices makes it appealing to enable many attractive applications. However, there is still a huge gap between it and its actual use. The main issues can be concludes as Environment-dependency, Sensitive to location and orientation, not robust to Multi-user scenario and Security issues. Since the current Wi-Fi devices are designed for communication, the limitation of the harware (especially the limited number of antenna and narrow bandwidth) makes the Wi-Fi sensing systems hard to break through these bottle necks.

A Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS), also known as an Intelligent Reflecting Surface (IRS), comprises an array of reflected antenna elements, each of which can independently incur some change to the incident signal. The rising of RIS provides us many potential solutions to solve these open issues. We propose that RIS can support three new capabilities to current Wi-Fi sensing systems. First, RIS provides a new and controllable transmission path to bypass obstacles. Second, it can also concentrate the wireless energy reflected by RIS to certain regions of interest (ROI). Third, RIS can proactively change the wireless environment, which makes it useful for attack applications.

To verify them in a real environment, we implemented a RIS prototype. As shown in the figure, our prototype comprises a RIS and a FPGA-based control circuit. The smart surface has 16 × 16 antenna elements with a size of 31𝑐𝑚 × 31𝑐𝑚 × 0.4𝑐𝑚. Each element has a 2-bit phase configuration, four different phase offsets (i.e., 0, 𝜋 /2, 𝜋 , and 3𝜋 /2 ). Its central frequency is 5.5GHz and it has a bandwidth of 600MHz, which makes the working band cover the whole 5GHz Wi-Fi band. The FPGA-based control circuit provides 512 independent IOs and its latency to update all the IO ports is at a microsecond level and guarantees the fast configuration switch.

What’s more, we propose RIScan, a parallel multi-user indoor localization system with a pair of Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces. In RIScan, we realize a localization system with low latency and high robustness to dynamic interference from surrounding people in multi-user scenario. Our key insight is to utilize the reconfigurability of RIS. To achieve low localization latency, we leverage RIS to reflect Wi-Fi signals in the environment and create steerable Wi-Fi beams. Then execute reflective beam scanning and the users can estimate their locations concurrently with a series of channel estimations. To be robust, we apply a CSI difference and phase pattern detection to separate the RIS component and suppress the dynamic interference in the environment. We deploy our system in a real environment and the experiments show that RIScan achieves a good performance.

Futhermore, we find it interesting that the new capabilities brought by RIS also pose challenges to the security of traditional Wi-Fi awareness systems. Now the WiFi signal has been leveraged by some commercial Intrusion Detection system to protect people. The system generates an alarm by detecting disturbances in the Wi-Fi channel caused by the movement of an intruder. Due to the capability of RIS to proactively change the wireless environment, we propose a two-phase attack that combines reflection reduction and threshold lifting, they are Sneaking Phase and Radio Blast Phase. Specifically, we use RIS to successfully confuse the intrusion detection system by first reflecting the intruder’s reflected signal to a place where the detection system’s receiver cannot detect it and then generating a directional disturbance signal to increase the system’s detection threshold.

Overall, the new capabilities provided by RIS give us a lot of room to solve the open issues in the field of RF sensing. We believe that the introduction of RIS will greatly shorten the distance between the RF sensing system and real users.
